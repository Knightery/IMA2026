## The Role of Data Analytics

Data-driven decision making is essential to all types of companies in their operations. Operations like sales, customer service, and manufacturing produce data, and companies also collect external data regarding competitors, market sectors, the economy, and the environment. This data can be structured, unstructured, quantitative, or qualitative. Analyzing this information is referred to as "data analytics" or "big data analytics". Depending on the decision being supported, data analytics utilizes descriptive, diagnostic, predictive, and prescriptive approaches.

Companies rely on platforms and tools to collect, store, and use data, which is referred to as "data governance". Data analytics helps organizations remain agile, cost-effective, and "risk intelligent" in the face of global challenges such as the COVID pandemic, supply chain disruptions, and geopolitical uncertainty. It helps frame problems, collect relevant data, analyze it, and make the results accessible via visualization tools. Managerial accountants heavily use data analytics to support decision-making. They face challenges including acquiring critical skills, staying abreast of technological changes, adapting communication strategies, and managing an exploding amount of data. Popular platforms like Tableau, Excel, SAP, and R are useful for conducting data analysis and visualization.

## West Valley Fresh Distribution Company

West Valley Fresh Distribution Company (West Valley) is based in Southern California. The company purchases fresh vegetables, fruits, and nuts and distributes them to U.S. retailers. California supplies many types of produce to U.S. consumers and imports produce from Mexico and other Latin American countries. The industry is highly competitive, particularly in California's Central Valley due to its proximity to transportation hubs and relatively low labor costs. Founded in 2015 in the southern Central Valley, West Valley has grown rapidly but faces constant challenges from competitors. The company must explore new opportunities and increase cost efficiency by analyzing markets, consumer behavior, and its own operations.

West Valley acts as an intermediary, purchasing directly from farms and selling to domestic retailers like restaurants and grocery stores. Their current product lines include fruits (cherries, grapes, oranges), nuts (almonds, pistachios, pecans), and vegetables (carrots, cauliflower, celery).

Recently, CEO Randy Miller considered carrying a new product line: organic Hass avocados. Avocados are increasingly popular in the U.S., particularly among the younger demographic, as they are considered very healthy. Hass avocados are a premier variety due to their high quality, nutrition, and taste. They are more expensive than other avocados due to supply and demand fluctuations, as well as cultivation challenges since they only grow well in certain regions. A 2018 USDA report showed that per capita consumption of avocados in the U.S. had tripled since 2001, projecting continued sales growth.

## The Hass Avocado Market Project

West Valley has no prior experience in the Hass avocado market. José Martínez, a recently hired cost accountant, is responsible for assisting the executive team in strategic decision-making. Randy asked José to investigate the Hass avocado market, analyze potential opportunities, and present a PowerPoint of his findings in one month. Though José grew up in California and likes avocados, he knows little about the Hass variety.

José faces three critical challenges:

* Gathering and analyzing supplier information.


* Preparing an overview of the U.S. Hass avocado market (sales, pricing, and major market data).


* Identifying potential opportunities and risks.



Concerned about his limited data analytics background, José plans to employ data visualization tools to generate graphical information to avoid a "dry" presentation.

Through a local agricultural business association, José learned that 90% of Hass avocados consumed in the U.S. are imported from Mexico, while nearly all the remaining 10% are grown in Southern California. A local farm is currently looking for buyers. José was advised to visit the Hass Avocado Board (HAB) website, where he downloaded weekly avocado sales data from January 2019 to November 2022 from 41 metropolitan regions at no cost.

To improve his analysis, José contacted Melanie Kirkland, CMA, a business consultant in business analytics and data visualization he met at a local IMA event. Melanie suggested ranking sales by city and year, studying seasonality effects, collecting cost information, simulating sales transactions, and conducting profit analysis.

José interviewed local farms and found they could supply 20,000 organic Hass avocados at $0.20 each this year. Transportation costs from the West Valley warehouse to regional stores would be $50 for every 100 miles plus $1,500 in fixed costs. José initially plans to recommend charging customers a wholesale price that is 40% of the local retail price to penetrate the market and maintain a profit margin. He will combine projected avocado costs, transportation, logistics, and operating expenses, comparing them to projected revenues to justify recommendations.

In a follow-up meeting, Randy was impressed by José's progress and the HAB historical data. Randy requested a comprehensive analysis including a market overview, price elasticity, and revenue/profit modeling using company and market data. Randy also asked him to investigate seasonal factors and how to address related risks. José has a few weeks to finish his analysis and prepare his presentation.
